S — Strategic Roadmapping

Everyday Goal Setting • The Process Goal Roadmap

Obsessing over results like a promotion, a top grade, or praise from everyone creates constant stress, because those outcomes depend on timing, other people's choices, and luck. Strategic Roadmapping means literally crossing out outcome goals, putting 100% of your attention on daily process goals you completely control, and sending positive ripples to the people around you.

Step 1 · Yourself

The Process Goal Roadmap

How to Apply It to Yourself: Every choice you make sends ripples through your day. Strategic Roadmapping means replacing old knee-jerk habits with intentional positive ripples—and focusing strictly on controllable Process Goals rather than stressful Outcome Goals.
• Outcome Goals (getting a big promotion, winning praise from everyone) sit outside your direct control and fuel anxiety.
• Process Goals (preparing thoroughly beforehand, staying calm and attentive, speaking clearly) are 100% within your control.

Step 2 · Others

Sending Positive Ripples

How to Apply It to Others: People around you feel the ripples of your choices immediately. When you drop your head or complain after an error, that negative ripple spreads tension across the whole room. When you step up promptly to help fix an issue or speak clearly and early ("I'm on this—here's our next step"), that positive ripple gives everyone confidence and calm direction.

You're part of a church group (or community committee) running a fundraiser dinner. Thirty minutes before doors open, the caterer calls to say they'll be an hour late. The room goes quiet. One volunteer groans, "Great, this whole night is ruined," and you can feel the tension spreading.

Step 4 · Your Summary

Your Roadmap Summary

🎯 Try This Today: Cross It Out & Ripple It Forward

  1. Cross it out: Write your biggest outcome goal on a piece of paper and physically draw a line through it.
  2. Pick one process goal for today that is 100% in your control (e.g., "I'll listen without interrupting in every conversation today").
  3. Rate it tonight: Give yourself 1–5 stars for how steadily you followed through. No judgment—just information.
  4. Send one positive ripple: When something goes wrong today—yours or someone else's—be the first person to say, "I've got you—let's take this step by step."
